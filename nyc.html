Richard Edes Harrison

The City of New York

July 1939 · Fortune Magazine

One of the most celebrated American maps of the twentieth century. Harrison's oblique aerial projection was revolutionary — presenting the city as if seen from an aircraft at a time when such a view was still extraordinary.

Published as a Fortune Magazine supplement in July 1939, it would go on to define wartime cartography in American media, establishing a new visual language for geographic representation.

The colour coding across boroughs and the extraordinary detail of Manhattan's street grid make this a document as much of urban planning as of cartography. Zoom in on lower Manhattan to appreciate the density of information Harrison packed into a single sheet.
