Chatelain · Carte de la Nouvelle France · 1719
About

The Holocene, our current geological epoch, began around 12,000 years ago. The stable climate it brought allowed humans to build civilizations and terraform the planet to their own design.

These maps illustrate that transformation. They were used by our ancestors to stake claims, assert dominance, and demonstrate power over contested lands — cartography as instrument of empire.

This collection focuses on a century of imperial struggle between France and Britain for North America, 1680–1780, from the height of French ambition to its final resolution.

The Maps

Act I · 1719

Henri Abraham Chatelain

Carte de la Nouvelle France, ou se voit le cours des grandes rivières

1719 · Amsterdam

This map was produced in 1719 by Henri Chatelain in his Atlas Historique, a commercial atlas aimed at educated European readers. Drawing on De l'Isle's authoritative 1718 cartography, it presents French territorial claims over the Mississippi basin and Great Lakes at the precise moment the Mississippi Bubble was at its peak.

Act II · c. 1740

Emanuel Bowen

A Map of the King of Great Britain's Dominions in Europe, Africa, and America

c. 1740 · London

Emanuel Bowen produced this map on the eve of the French and Indian War, making British imperial claim its explicit subject. Yellow outlining traces the dominions of the King of Great Britain across three continents — Europe, Africa, and America — presenting the Atlantic not as an ocean dividing possessions but as a British medium connecting them.

Act III · 1763

John Gibson · The Gentleman's Magazine

The British Governments in North America, Laid Down Agreeable to the Proclamation of October 7, 1763

1763 · London

Published to accompany the Royal Proclamation of October 7, 1763, this map codifies Britain's victory over France in the most direct cartographic terms. New administrative boundaries carve the former French territories into provinces, and a vast expanse labeled Lands Reserved for the Indians marks the Proclamation Line — the boundary beyond which colonial settlement was forbidden.
